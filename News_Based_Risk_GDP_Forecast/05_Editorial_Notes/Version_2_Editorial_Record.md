# Version 2: news-risk and GDP information-set evaluation

## Editorial purpose

Version 2 restores the newspaper-based GRE indicator as an integral part of the empirical question while retaining the Denton weight analysis as the explanation for why forecast rankings can depend on retrospective GDP construction.

## Manuscript changes

- Replaced the title and abstract with a formulation that identifies the newspaper-based signal and the constructed-target evaluation together.
- Rewrote the introduction around the economic question, the two connected contributions, the limits of the empirical design, and a flowchart of the complete measurement/evaluation process.
- Recast Section 3 as **News-Based Risk Measurement and Forecast Evaluation**. It now states the frozen article-level score, country-month aggregation, calibration period, coverage, language limitation, and role of GRE relative to the broader AERI/ARS measurement system.
- Added a signal-construction table and descriptive GRE figure to the main manuscript, with separate display notes.
- Retained the Denton proposition and proof, Monte Carlo evidence, forecast tables, inference, and the previously reported numeric estimates.
- Rewrote the conclusion to connect the contribution of the news signal to the information-set analysis.
- Updated the supporting-information and title-page headings. The supplement continues to document article dictionaries, source coverage, data quality, technical detail, and robustness checks.

## Important boundaries

The reported GRE signal is the Growth/Real Economy component of a news-risk measurement framework, not a direct GDP measure, a full validation of AERI/ARS, or an estimate of unique news events. The production GRE dictionaries supplied for replication explicitly activate English, French, and Portuguese terms, and no separate Arabic-script GRE activation dictionary is documented. The source registry itself contains Arabic-language outlets.

The exact geometric-tail result concerns the interior bounded infinite-block additive first-difference Denton operator on monthly **levels**. Its finite-sample analogue is not exact. Changes in forecast loss have no determined sign.

The publication-lagged design changes admissible benchmark years and the entry date of training labels, but uses final-vintage GDP amounts and final-vintage values of admitted training labels. It is not a complete historical-vintage backtest.

The reported results have **not** been recomputed for Version 2. The additions clarify measurement and positioning; there are no new predictive-validity estimates or tests of alternative article-level dictionaries, severity tiers, or weighting coefficients.
